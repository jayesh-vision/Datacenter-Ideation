SynapseDC · Technical specification

Northbound and southbound interfaces

How the SynapseDC datacenter platform exchanges data with the systems above it (portals, ITSM, BSS, analytics, SIEM, the other platform modules) and with the equipment and systems below it (network, servers and BMCs, GPUs, power, cooling, BMS, access control), through the on-site collector. Every interface lists its direction, protocol, port, authentication, rate and payload.

Target design · v0.9 draft · 27-Oct-2026Contract: app/src/api/types.tsRegister: INT-01 to INT-28 and NB-01 to NB-12

1Purpose, scope and the interface map

SynapseDC sits between the datacenter's equipment and the business. Southbound interfaces bring live state and records in from the equipment and the site systems; they are read-only. Northbound interfaces give the systems above SynapseDC its data and events (APIs, event streams, ticket and paging sync, exports, embedded screens). East–west interfaces connect SynapseDC to the other modules of the platform (Operate to Acquire / BSS, Services & ITSM, Plan to Build, Supply Chain, Opstral Ops Toolkit).

Northbound what SynapseDC offers

REST API v1 (queries and commands), events (webhooks, Kafka), ITSM and paging sync, CRM / billing exchange, metric and bulk export, SIEM feed, embedded screens and a tenant-scoped API.

Southbound what SynapseDC reads

Live state from equipment through one collector per site (SNMP, Redfish, gNMI, Modbus, BACnet, DCGM, schedulers, PACS) and records from DCIM, CMDB, IPAM and OEM systems. Never writes to plant, security equipment or tenant gear.

Status. This is the target design. The prototype implements the API contract (api/types.ts) over mock data; the gateway, event bus, collector, stores and connectors are to be built. Items marked to discuss need a decision with the customer or a platform module team.

2Design principles

  1. Southbound is read-only. SynapseDC never switches, sets or resets plant, BMS points, network configuration, BMCs, security equipment or tenant equipment. Recommendations are carried out by people, through a change request.
  2. Outbound only from the site. The collector opens every connection; nothing outside the datacenter connects in. Protocols that must not cross a WAN (SNMP, Modbus, BACnet, IPMI) stay on the site's management and facility VLANs.
  3. One contract. Every screen, API response and event uses the shapes in api/types.ts (Alert, Incident, Change, NetworkElement, Datacenter, Service, Customer, GpuNode…). Vendor formats are converted at the collector or the connector, never in the core.
  4. The platform module owns its record. Where a platform module (CRM, ITSM, Inventory, IP Management, Vendors) holds an object, it is the system of record; SynapseDC reads it and links to it. Where the customer has their own system, it is integrated; where they have none, the platform's module is offered.
  5. Idempotent and replayable. Every write carries an Idempotency-Key; every event has an id and a per-object sequence; consumers can replay from a cursor or Kafka offset.
  6. Versioned. URL major version (/v1); additive changes only within a version; deprecations announced with Deprecation and Sunset headers at least 6 months ahead.
  7. Time and identity. All times are UTC, RFC 3339 in JSON (the prototype uses epoch ms internally); every sample keeps the source and receipt timestamps. Object ids are stable strings (MH-DC-001, INC-0001, CHG-2001); external references are kept in externalRefs[].
  8. Customers' data stays theirs. For colocation, tenant equipment is never polled (power and environment at the cabinet only). For a GPU cloud, GPU telemetry and job metadata are read; workload data, models and code never are.

Webhook delivery

  • Subscriptions: POST /webhooks with url, types[], optional filter (dcId, severity), and a secret.
  • Headers: webhook-id, webhook-timestamp, webhook-signature: v1,<base64 HMAC-SHA256(id.timestamp.body)> (Standard Webhooks). Reject when the timestamp is older than 5 minutes.
  • Receiver answers 2xx within 10 s. Otherwise retries at 10 s, 1 min, 5 min, 30 min, 2 h, 6 h, then 12-hourly for 3 days; after that the subscription is paused and an alarm is raised. Delivery is at-least-once: de-duplicate on id, order per subject by sequence.
  • Replay: POST /webhooks/:id/replay?from=<time>; the 7-day log is in GET /webhooks/:id/deliveries.

Kafka

Topics above, key = subject (keeps one object's events in order), value = the CloudEvent (structured mode), retention 7 days (alerts 30). Consumers authenticate with SASL/SCRAM-SHA-512 or mTLS and get read ACLs per topic and prefix. Schemas are registered (JSON Schema); a breaking change gets a new topic version (.v2).

15The on-site collector South

One collector per datacenter (two for Tier III / IV: active and standby) on the site's management network is the only thing that talks to the equipment. It holds the site's credentials, runs the protocol readers, converts readings and events into the contract shapes, buffers them and sends them out.

Form

VM on the customer's virtualisation (8 vCPU, 16 GB RAM, 200 GB SSD for a 2,000-device site) or a 1U appliance in a management rack. Linux, containers, signed images.

Reach

Management VLAN (network, BMC), facility / BMS VLAN (UPS, PDUs, meters, BACnet), GPU management (DCGM exporters, scheduler). One interface per VLAN or a routed, firewalled path.

Readers

Prometheus snmp_exporter / Telegraf (SNMP, Modbus, Redfish sensors, gNMI, IPMI), our Redfish inventory and event client, BACnet client or the BMS's OPC UA server, DCGM scrape, scheduler / UFM / PACS connectors.

Credentials

Encrypted vault on the collector (rotated, never sent to the core); SNMPv3 users, Redfish accounts, API tokens are created read-only by the customer.

Lifecycle

  1. Enrol: one-time token from SynapseDC → the collector creates a key pair and receives a client certificate (mTLS) bound to its site.
  2. Configure: it pulls its job list (targets, credentials references, intervals, mapping tables) over the same outbound channel; changes are versioned.
  3. Run: poll, receive traps / events, normalise, buffer, send. A heartbeat every 60 s carries version, queue depth, targets OK / failing.
  4. Fail over: the standby takes the targets when the active misses 3 heartbeats; traps and syslog are sent to a virtual IP or to both.
  5. Upgrade: signed release pulled from the platform; rolled back automatically if the heartbeat does not return.

20Normalisation: from a vendor event to an Alert South

  1. Parse the trap, syslog line, Redfish event, BACnet notification or threshold crossing with the vendor mapping table.
  2. Identify: identity = source + node + resource + alarm type (as ServiceNow message_key / PagerDuty dedup_key). A repeat updates last-seen and count; a clear closes it. Sources with no clear close after two quiet intervals. More than 3 raise / clear cycles in 15 min = held open, flapping.
  3. Grade with the severity table (configuration, not code).
  4. Place on the record tree (site → floor → room → rack → device → port) by address or serial; add rackId for a rack-level fault and fault.id for one physical fault seen on several devices.
  5. Scope: site, edge, dependency or downstream (downstream is never counted, only shown as symptomOf).
  6. Keep both the normalised alarm and the raw event (audit, replay).

22Security

  • In transit: TLS 1.3 everywhere off-site; mTLS collector ↔ platform; SNMPv3 authPriv; Redfish HTTPS; BACnet/SC where the BMS supports it. Protocols without security (Modbus, BACnet/IP, SNMP v1/v2c) stay on isolated VLANs reached only by the collector.
  • Least privilege: read-only accounts on every source; separate OAuth clients and scopes per northbound integration; write scopes only for ITSM, paging and notices, and for the DCIM / IPAM records SynapseDC owns.
  • OT zoning: the collector sits in a conduit between the IT management zone and the facility zone (IEC 62443 zones and conduits); it accepts no inbound sessions.
  • Secrets: site credentials in the collector vault; northbound secrets in the platform secret store; rotation at least every 90 days.
  • At rest: stores encrypted (AES-256); per-tenant separation; region chosen by the customer (data residency).
  • Audit: every API write, record change and integration call is in the audit trail and the SIEM feed.

24Onboarding and acceptance of an interface

  1. Agree the row in the register: owner, direction, system of record, scopes, rate.
  2. Credentials and firewall rules raised by the customer (section 21).
  3. Connect in the sandbox tenant; replay sample payloads; check mappings (severity, fields, ids).
  4. Contract tests: the OpenAPI / JSON Schema checks pass for every payload in both directions.
  5. Reconcile: counts in SynapseDC equal counts in the source (devices, alarms, incidents, customers).
  6. Failure drills: source down, WAN down (buffer and replay), receiver down (webhook retries), duplicate events.
  7. Sign-off with the customer owner; the site integration survey records it (Help & FAQ → Facility integration).

25Open decisions

  1. System of record per object: SynapseDC / platform Inventory as the DCIM, or synced from the customer's NetBox / dcTrack / Nlyte / CMDB.
  2. GPU job progress (step time, MFU, checkpoints): a metrics endpoint or log export agreed with the AI platform team.
  3. BMS access: read-only BACnet or OPC UA, or relay contacts only (a facility-safety decision for the customer).
  4. PACS and visitor vendor per customer (one connector each).
  5. Tenant-scoped API (NB-12): scope and whether it is offered through the platform's Experience and Care module.
  6. SQL access for BI: direct views or export only.
  7. Retention periods and data residency per customer.
  8. Platform modules for facility live state, twin, capacity, revenue and RCA (today held in SynapseDC).
