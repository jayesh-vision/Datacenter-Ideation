1Executive summary

Datacenter operators run their estate on separate tools: a BMS for the building, an NMS for the network, a DCIM for records, a ticketing tool for incidents, spreadsheets for capacity and billing. None of them answers the questions that matter in the first minutes of a fault: what is failing, who is affected, what is it costing, and what should we do first? For a GPU cloud the gap is sharper still: a single failed GPU can stop a 152-GPU training job, and nothing today joins the facility, the fabric, the GPU and the job into one picture.

SynapseDC closes that gap on top of the platform the customer already runs. It adds the datacenter-specific capabilities the platform does not have — the live facility (power, cooling, environment), the physical last mile (which UPS feeds which rack, which cable carries which service), the GPU fleet and its jobs, datacenter alarm scope and correlation, the revenue impact of every open issue, capacity and power economics, a planner for new capacity, DCIM records for racks, power chains and cabling, datacenter security operations and compliance — and it uses the platform's existing modules for everything they already own: CRM, customers, orders and catalogue (Operate to Acquire / BSS), incidents, changes, tickets, maintenance and knowledge (Services & ITSM), alerts, telemetry and security tooling (Opstral Ops Toolkit), AI models (AI Foundry), customisation (Platform Engineering), the data layer (Intelligence Hub) and inventory, IP management, programs and projects (Plan to Build).

First release targets two business models: colocation (the operator sells power, space and cooling; tenant equipment is never looked into) and NeoCloud / GPU cloud (the operator rents GPUs and must keep expensive jobs running). Both share one estate model, one alarm model and one money model; what differs is vocabulary, what is monitored and how value is counted. Cloud, enterprise and telco variants follow.

2Business context

2.1Problem statement

  1. Fragmented view. Facility, network, compute, GPU, security and commercial data live in different tools with different identities. Nobody can see one site end to end.
  2. Impact is guessed. When a UPS goes on battery or a backhaul link drops, the services and customers affected — and the money at risk — are worked out by hand, after the event.
  3. GPU operations are blind to the building. GPU clouds see Xid errors and job states, but not that the CDU flow dropped or a rack was power-capped. Facility teams see the CDU, not the job.
  4. Capacity decisions are slow. Where to place the next 256 GPUs, what a new hall costs, when power runs out — answered with spreadsheets and long cycles.
  5. The physical last mile is undocumented. Which PSU is on which PDU outlet, which trunk carries which service, which cabinet is single-fed: unknown until a fault proves it.
  6. Power is the largest cost and the least managed. Take-or-pay floors, demand ratchets and idle colocation blocks are found at invoice time.

2.3Success criteria for the first release

  • A colocation operator and a NeoCloud operator run the Operate core (section 12, P1) on at least one live site each, with the collector connected to the BMS, UPS, PDUs, network, BMCs and (NeoCloud) DCGM and the scheduler.

3Scope and the module split

3.1Principle

The platform module that already owns an object is its system of record. SynapseDC builds only what is datacenter-specific on top of it, uses the module's data through its API or the platform event bus, and never duplicates its screens. Where a customer runs their own system instead of the platform module (ServiceNow, Salesforce, NetBox…), the same datacenter capabilities integrate with that system.

3.3Out of scope (all releases unless re-decided)

6.2Architecture

  • On-site collector (one per datacenter, active + standby) reads the equipment read-only (SNMPv3, gNMI, syslog, Redfish, IPMI, Modbus, BACnet / OPC UA, DCGM, scheduler, UFM, PACS), normalises alarms, buffers and sends outbound only over mTLS 443 (interface spec INT-01…13, INT-28).
  • Central platform: ingest (OTLP / remote write / events), stores (time series, events, records), read models (impact, revenue, RCA, GPU, physical, capacity, power economics, security, compliance), the API (REST v1 — the same contract the screens use), the event bus (CloudEvents on webhooks / Kafka) and connectors to platform modules and customer systems (NB-01…12, EW).
  • Screens run in the platform shell or embedded in the portal (shell=false) with the platform's identity (OIDC).
  • Data: every count is derived from entities; nothing is typed in. Every sample keeps source and receipt time; fresh = younger than two polling intervals.

6.3Key design decisions (carried from the prototype)

IDDecision
DD-01An alarm counts against a datacenter only when in scope: site, edge (counted at both ends), dependency; downstream is never counted, only shown as a symptom under the causing incident.
DD-02Every impacted service is charged to exactly one issue (its most severe cause); money reconciles across issues, causes, datacenters, customers and sites.
DD-03Health is shown with LEDs, beacons, halos and chips; cabinets and plant stay neutral. Red / amber only for faults.
DD-04Every DCIM write runs under a change request: standard (pre-approved record work), an open CR at the site, or raise new.

7Modules built by SynapseDC

Each chapter follows the same layout: purpose · scope · business rules · functional requirements · user stories and acceptance criteria · data · integrations · reports and KPIs · screens.

7.1Command Center

Purpose. One landing screen that tells the operations lead, in under a minute, how healthy the whole datacenter estate is, which sites are in trouble, who is affected and what to do next. Every figure on it is clickable and opens the list that produced it, with the same count. Lifecycle stage: Operate (with a Plan & Build strip for the build pipeline). Primary users: NOC / operations manager, datacenter site manager, head of operations, executive sponsor. Builds on: new (the existing Intelligence Hub keeps its own dashboards; this Command Center is datacenter-specific). Reads incidents from Services & ITSM, alarms from Ops Toolkit System Alerts, customers from BSS. Colocation: the first health area is Incident response; "premium customers" are premium tenants; tenant cages are not GPU fleet. NeoCloud: the first health area is GPU fleet (GPUs healthy and at full speed ÷ installed), job stalls drive the status, GPU time lost per hour is shown.

7.1.1Scope

  • In scope: estate health ring and verdict, four health areas, clickable headline numbers, live estate map, datacenter column (worst first), What is happening · Who is affected · Recommendations, Plan & build strip and build lens, info text on every widget.
  • Out of scope: incident and change records themselves (Services & ITSM Incidents / Change Management); customer master data (BSS CRM / Customers); generic platform dashboards (Intelligence Hub); revenue detail (7.6 Revenue Impact).

7.1.4User stories and acceptance criteria

US-CC-01 As a NOC manager I want one verdict and four areas so that I know where to look first (CC-001, CC-002, CC-005).

  • Given a UPS is on battery at one site, when I open the Command Center, then Power shows Critical even if its score is above 95 % and the hover names the UPS.

US-CC-02 As a NOC manager I want every number to open its list so that I can trust the dashboard (CC-003, CC-004, CC-021).

  • Given the High tile shows 8, when I click it, then the incidents list opens filtered to open High incidents and shows 8 rows.

US-CC-03 As a NeoCloud operations lead I want GPU fleet first so that the product's health leads (CC-008, CC-010).

  • Given a training job is stalled by an Xid 79 GPU, then GPU fleet is Critical, the detail names the GPUs stopped and the money lost per hour, and the site chip opens that site's jobs.

US-CC-04 As a colocation site manager I want Incident response first so that tenant-facing response leads (CC-009).

  • Given a colocation estate with no operator GPUs, then the first area is Incident response with % within SLA.

US-CC-05 As an executive I want to see who is affected so that I can call key customers (CC-018).

  • Given three premium customers are affected, then the premium tile shows 3 and clicking scrolls to Who is affected with those three first.

US-CC-06 As an operations engineer I want the map to show where trouble is (CC-011, CC-012, CC-014).

  • Given a site has a Critical incident, then its pin ripples; when I search its city and pick it, the map flies to it and opens its card.

US-CC-07 As a planning lead I want the build pipeline on the landing page (CC-022, CC-023).

  • Given two plans await approval, then the strip shows 2 decisions due and Recommendations lists them for their approvers.

US-CC-08 As a platform user I want the screen embedded in the portal (CC-032).

  • Given the portal frames the Command Center with shell off, then no menu or top bar shows and links stay inside the frame.

US-CC-09 As a NOC manager I want to know what to do first (CC-035).

  • Given a Critical incident with a pending action and an emergency change awaiting approval, then Recommendations lists both at rank 1, each with its reason and benefit, and clicking opens the incident or the change.

US-CC-10 As a head of operations I want to ask the assistant (CC-031).

  • Given I ask "Which issue is costing the most revenue?", then the answer names the issue, its money per hour and links to it, and the figure equals the Revenue impact screen.

7.3Facilities — power, cooling, environment & safety, single points of failure, maintenance

Purpose. Shows how power and cooling reach every rack, whether the site can ride through a utility failure, where one failure would take something down, and when the plant is next maintained. IDRule
BRULE-FAC-01Power chain: utility feed → ATS → UPS module (A side = UPS-2/4, B side = UPS-1/3) → busway per room → rack PDU A / B → breaker bank → outlet → PSU. Each PDU side is rated at the rack budget.
BRULE-FAC-02Odd PSUs connect to PDU A, even to PDU B; Σ PDU load = rack draw.

BRULE-FAC-05Each rack has a nearest cooling unit; liquid racks send 75 % of their heat to their CDU.
BRULE-FAC-06Single points of failure: both cords on A, failed PSU, tripped PDU, cabinet that one side cannot carry (≥ 90 % of budget), UPS not online, Tier II single feed / tray route / building entry, cooling spare lost.
BRULE-FAC-07What-if assets: feed, UPS, side AB, PDU, cooling unit, trunk; result = devices, racks, services and customers that lose power, cooling or connectivity.

BRULE-FAC-11Leak detection: rope under cooling units, CDUs and liquid racks. Fire zone per room with detection (VESDA / point), suppression (Novec / IG-541 / pre-action), quarterly inspection.
BRULE-FAC-12PUE = facility power ÷ IT power from the site's power split (IT, cooling, lighting & other).

BRULE-FAC-17What-if on a utility feed drops nothing: the UPS and the DG carry it, so it is shown as a ride-through. What-if on a UPS takes every PDU it feeds; on a PDU its outlets; on a cooling unit its zone's spare; on a trunk every link in it.
BRULE-FAC-18PM intervals: UPS inspection and thermal scan, cooling filter and coil, CDU coolant and filter, fire inspection every 91 days; battery and DG tests on their own dates; a booked Maintenance change counts as scheduled, not overdue.

7.4GPU Fleet

Purpose. Show every GPU cluster, node and customer job the operator runs. For each one, show whether it is healthy and earning, what is slowing or stopping it, and what that costs in GPU time, power and work to redo. This is the product's main use case: a GPU estate is the most expensive and the most fault-sensitive hardware in the building. Lifecycle stage: Operate (with Plan inputs for placement and power cost). Primary users: GPU / AI platform operations engineer, NOC engineer, facility engineer (liquid cooling), capacity planner, customer success manager, operations head. Builds on: new. It uses Opstral Telemetry Ops and Infra Ops pipelines for collection, and the BSS Customers / Orders records for job ownership and price.

Colocation: the GPUs belong to the tenant and are never polled. GPU Fleet shows only the tenant AI cages: high-density cabinets (40–60 kW), contracted kW against draw, the liquid loop (CDU) feeding them, inlet temperature and state. There are no jobs, nodes or DCGM telemetry. The menu item is shown only when the estate has GPU infrastructure. NeoCloud: full scope. Operator-owned GPUs, per-GPU DCGM-style telemetry, and customer jobs as the scheduler sees them (job, customer, GPUs and nodes, state, MFU, checkpoint). Workload data, models and code are never read.

7.4.1Scope

In scope

  • Clusters / pods (at most 32 nodes each, one GPU model per pod), nodes, GPUs and hot spares.
  • Jobs (training, fine-tuning, inference) with their states and the money effect of each state.
  • Per-GPU telemetry following the NVIDIA DCGM exporter fields, plus the Intel Gaudi (hl-smi) and Intel XPU Manager equivalents.
  • GPU models and vendors catalogue (NVIDIA B200, H200, H100, A100, L40S, T4; Intel Gaudi 3, Data Center GPU Max 1550).
  • Network rails: InfiniBand (400G NDR), Ethernet front-end (100G), Management / OOB (1G), with port and error state.
  • Cooling and power for GPU racks: liquid loops (CDU), rack draw against budget, power cost per GPU-hour, energy and power cost by job and by tenant.
  • Where to place the next job: a ranked list of pods.
  • "Jobs needing attention" story cards (biggest blast radius, slowest job).
  • Node detail and the device "GPUs · DCGM" view.

Out of scope

  • Job scheduling and submission: the customer's Slurm / Kubernetes / Run:ai.
  • Billing of GPU hours: the customer billing system. The BSS Plans & Products Catalog holds the GPU product and rate.
  • Ticketing and on-call: Services & ITSM Incidents and Tickets, Ops Toolkit System Alerts.
  • Model / training-framework internals, and any customer data.

7.4.2Business rules

IDRule
BRULE-GPU-01Job states are Running, Slowed, Stalled, At risk and Idle. Stalled means the job has stopped because one of its GPUs or nodes failed (for example Xid 79, GPU fell off the bus): every rank waits at the step barrier. Slowed means the step time is above the baseline (an NVLink straggler, thermal or hardware slowdown from low coolant flow, an InfiniBand rail flapping); MFU falls by the same factor. At risk means the job still runs but a node in it carries a fault where the next event crashes it (uncorrectable ECC with a row remap pending). Idle means the job holds an allocation but has run below 5 % utilisation for 6 hours or more; no alarm is raised for it (it is waste, not a fault) and it is shown on the jobs list and the Command Center GPU area instead. Job kinds are training, fine-tuning and inference; inference-only models (L40S, T4) run inference jobs only.
BRULE-GPU-02Job price per hour = GPUs × (persona rate per GPU-month ÷ 730) × the model's price factor (H100 = H200 = 1; B200 1.45, A100 0.55, L40S 0.38, T4 0.08, Gaudi 3 0.6, Max 1550 0.45). The NeoCloud reference rate is ₹1,50,000 per GPU-month (cloud list ₹1,75,000, enterprise charge-back ₹85,000 for the later variants); the customer's own product rate from the catalogue replaces it. The same figure is the job's service charge for Revenue Impact.
BRULE-GPU-03GPU time lost now (per hour) = the full job price per hour when Stalled or Idle; the job price per hour × the share of step time lost when Slowed (share = 1 − baseline ÷ current step time); 0 when Running.
BRULE-GPU-04Lost so far = the lost-now rate × hours since the state began. The fleet's "lost so far" total adds the work to redo of stalled jobs (BRULE-GPU-05).
BRULE-GPU-05Work to redo (Stalled job) = the job price per hour × hours between the last checkpoint and the stall.
BRULE-GPU-06At stake (At-risk job) = the job price per hour × the checkpoint interval in hours (the work lost if it crashes now).
BRULE-GPU-07Node states are In a job, Spare (idle, about 10 % of each pod kept as hot spares), Drained, Failed (open RMA; the failed GPU shows Xid 79 for NVIDIA parts) and Burn-in (24 h after a repair). A GPU counts as healthy and at full speed unless it is in a Stalled / Slowed / At-risk job, failed, degraded, or throttled. Spares count as healthy; drained, failed and burn-in nodes are out of service. Fleet health % = healthy ÷ installed; the Command Center GPU fleet area shows this figure with the detail "N GPUs stopped by one failed GPU · N jobs slowed · money lost per hour".
BRULE-GPU-08Fleet status is Critical when any job is Stalled. It is Attention when any job is Slowed or At risk, any allocation is Idle, any GPU is throttled, or any GPU has failed. Otherwise it is Normal.
BRULE-GPU-09Throttle reasons are taken from the DCGM throttle field and reported as thermal, hardware slowdown or power cap.
BRULE-GPU-10Xid buckets: 48, 64, 79 and 95 = reset the GPU / restart the node (Critical). 74 = NVLink (Major). 94 = contained (Minor). 63 = ignored.
BRULE-GPU-11Driver drift = a node running its model's older driver while the pod's baseline is the current driver.
BRULE-GPU-12Power cost per installed GPU-hour = kW per GPU × site PUE × energy tariff. Power cost per sold GPU-hour = the same power spread over GPUs that are earning (in a running job). Both are compared with the model's price to show power share % and "left after power".
BRULE-GPU-13Job / tenant power cost per day = Σ node kW × PUE × tariff × 24. An idle allocation is billed, draws power and does no work. A power cap reports kW saved against the GPU time it costs.
BRULE-GPU-14Placement fit for N GPUs of a model is judged per pod on: free healthy GPUs, rack power headroom, cooling state (CDU for liquid racks), contracted power headroom, "already paid" kW (an idle block, or demand below the take-or-pay floor) and the marginal power cost per GPU-hour. Fit is Good, Tight or No.
BRULE-GPU-15Each job is a customer service on every node of the job, so a fault on any of its nodes affects that service and appears in Revenue Impact under the GPU domain (see 7.6).
BRULE-GPU-16Telemetry is read-only. The platform never resets a GPU, drains a node or changes a power cap. It recommends these, and people carry them out through a change.
BRULE-GPU-17Pods are at most 32 nodes of 8 GPUs, one GPU model per pod; the GPU host takes the pod's server model (NVIDIA, Supermicro or Dell OEM). Liquid-cooled halls have one CDU loop per three GPU racks (capacity = load × 1.6 rounded up to 50 kW; nominal flow = capacity × 1.45 L/min); liquid nodes report no fan. Cluster state is bad when a job in it is stalled or a GPU is failed in a job, warn when any GPU needs attention, else ok.
BRULE-GPU-18Reference scenarios the acceptance tests use (seeded on NVIDIA training pods): Xid 79 stalls the biggest training job at the GPU-fault site, last checkpoint 52 min before; Xid 48 row remap pending puts a fine-tune At risk; Xid 74 NVLink errors make one node a straggler and the job 22 % slower; CDU flow low (pump A seized) puts liquid-cooled GPUs into hardware slowdown with a Cooling incident; an InfiniBand rail flapping slows a job 12 % with no incident on purpose (the job is slowed, nothing is down); a rack near its breaker runs its GPUs power-capped at 550 W (Warning alarm, 6 h); an inference service has held its GPUs idle for 6 h (no alarm). A driver rollout change covers the nodes on the old driver.

7.4.7Reports and KPIs

  • Fleet health % = healthy and full-speed GPUs ÷ installed GPUs.
  • GPU utilisation in jobs = mean SM active over GPUs in a running job.
  • GPU time lost per hour = Σ lost-now per job (BRULE-GPU-03); split stalled / slowed / idle.
  • Work to redo = Σ job price per hour × hours since the last checkpoint (stalled jobs).
  • At stake = Σ job price per hour × checkpoint interval (at-risk jobs).
  • Allocation share = GPUs in a job ÷ installed GPUs; idle allocation GPUs.
  • Energy 24 h (MWh), average power as % of TDP.

7.5Datacenter alarm scope, correlation and incident enrichment

Purpose. Turn the raw alarms from every source into one normalised, de-duplicated stream that is placed on the datacenter. Decide whether each alarm counts against the site. Group related alarms into incidents enriched with impact, ranked root-cause hypotheses (with change evidence), recommended actions and verification. The alarm and the incident remain records of the existing platform modules; SynapseDC adds the datacenter intelligence. Lifecycle stage: Operate. Primary users: NOC engineer, facility engineer, incident manager, on-call engineer, operations head. Builds on: Ops Toolkit System Alerts (alarm record, routing, paging), Services & ITSM Incidents, Problems and Change Management (records, workflow, approvals), BSS Experience and Care (customer notices).

Colocation: tenant gear is never polled, so tenant-cabinet alarms come only from power (PDU, breaker, cabinet over contracted power) and environment (inlet, leak, door). A tenant over its own power budget is an alarm and a commercial matter, not a datacenter fault. Cross-connect and remote-hands tickets sit beside incidents. NeoCloud: GPU alarms (Xid, ECC, NVLink, throttle, CDU) are a domain of their own (incident type GPU). A job's service is hit by a fault on any of its nodes. Fabric alarms are reported by plane (InfiniBand, Ethernet front-end, OOB).

7.6Revenue Impact

Purpose. Show what every open datacenter issue is costing now, and what it puts at stake: revenue not delivered, SLA credits triggered, the next credit cliff and a 24-hour forecast. 7.7Datacenter Security Ops

Purpose. Give the datacenter SOC one view of security across the network, management (BMC / OOB), compute and Kubernetes, facility OT and physical access, in the datacenter context: change windows, DCIM placement, access log. This covers detections, cases, threat coverage, vulnerabilities, hardening and drift, OT zones and physical protection, mapped to industry standards. Lifecycle stage: Operate. Primary users: SOC analyst, SOC engineer, security manager / CISO, facility security, compliance officer. Builds on: Ops Toolkit Security Ops (SOC platform, case record, playbook engine). 7.8Capacity and power economics

Purpose. This module shows how much space, power, cooling, ports and GPU capacity each datacenter has: what is used, what is held against it, and what is truly free. It also shows what power costs under the operator's tariff and power contract. It answers three questions. Can we take the next customer or job? Where do we run out first? How much are we paying for power we do not use? Lifecycle stage: Plan / Operate. Primary users: capacity planner, facilities manager, commercial / sales engineer, finance (energy cost), GPU platform owner. Builds on: Plan to Build Inventory Planning (capacity plans and records), Plan to Build Inventory (racks, devices), BSS Customers (contracted kW). Colocation: capacity is sold as space and contracted kW per cabinet. Contracted kW above the cabinet's draw counts as a committed hold. Tenant power density and power over contract are the key figures. NeoCloud: capacity is sold as GPUs. The GPU view splits the fleet into in a job, allocated to an idle job, free and out of service, per GPU model. Power economics gives the power cost per installed and per sold GPU-hour, and shows where the next job can run on power that is already paid for.

7.8.1Scope

  • In:
  • capacity per datacenter, hall and dimension (space U, power kW, cooling kW, ports, GPUs);
  • reservations and holds from every source;
  • net available capacity;
  • forecast against the 85 % warning line, with approved and executing plans netted in;
  • network bandwidth and port headroom (links in countdown);
  • the financial view: CapEx approved and committed, OpEx per month, per site;
  • PUE, energy per day and CO₂;
  • power tariffs and power contracts per site, and the monthly power bill (energy and capacity charges);
  • idle committed kW and its cost, and cost per sold MW-month;
  • GPU power cost per GPU-hour, per job and per tenant;
  • power findings against the contracts, which raise alarms and incidents;
  • GPU job placement ranking (where to place the next job).
  • Out:
  • capacity plan records and approvals (Plan to Build Inventory Planning, through the Planner, see 7.9);
  • customer contracts and their contracted kW (BSS Customers / CRM, read);
  • billing of power to tenants (customer billing system; we only send metered usage, NB-07);
  • renewables share and WUE (not modelled, not shown).

7.9Planner

Purpose. The Planner turns a capacity need into a designed, costed, scheduled and approved plan:

  • a rack, pod or hall fit-out;
  • a plant upgrade or a network extension;
  • a new datacenter or an expansion;
  • a refresh or a migration.

It sizes the plan from templates and benchmarks, checks it against rules, compares scenarios and routes it for approval by CapEx band. It then hands the approved plan to Programs & Projects for delivery. Lifecycle stage: Plan → Build handoff. Primary users: capacity planner, design authority, finance manager, facilities director, executive approver, programme manager. Builds on:

  • Plan to Build Inventory Planning (plan record and holds);
  • Plan to Build Inventory and IP Management (site baseline, addresses);
  • Supply Chain Materials, Vendors, Contracts and Warehouses (catalogue, stock, vendor contracts);
  • Programs & Projects (delivery record).

Colocation: typical plans are a cage or hall fit-out for a tenant, a power upgrade, a new hall, or rack replacement. Sizing is in contracted kW per cabinet. NeoCloud: typical plans are a GPU pod or hall (liquid cooling and CDUs, InfiniBand fabric), a plant upgrade for high-density racks, a server refresh, or a new site. Sizing is in GPUs, kW per rack and cooling type.

7.9.1Scope

  • In:
  • plan families and their plan types:
  • in-site: rack, pod, hall, retrofit;
  • plant;
  • network;
  • build: new datacenter, expansion, portfolio, edge;
  • replace: server refresh, network refresh, rack replacement;
  • migration;
  • templates with inheritance, versions and variants;
  • benchmarks (costs, efficiency, lead times, sizing and staffing, rules);

7.10DCIM — racks, power chain and cabling

Purpose. DCIM holds the physical model that monitoring, impact and planning depend on:

  • where every rack and device sits;
  • how power reaches each device (UPS → busway or panel → PDU → outlet → PSU);
  • how every cable runs (port → patch panel → trunk → ODF → cross-connect).

These datacenter-specific records extend the Plan to Build Inventory asset record. They are changed only under a change request. Lifecycle stage: Build / Operate. Primary users: datacenter engineer, field technician, capacity planner, NOC (trace), colocation operations. Builds on: Plan to Build Inventory (asset record: site, device, serial, model, lifecycle), Services & ITSM Change Management (change requests). Colocation: tenant cabinets hold opaque "Tenant gear" (owner, tenant, contracted kW; never polled). The floor plan's Tenant colouring compares draw with contracted kW. Cross-connects and meet-me-room ODFs are central. NeoCloud: GPU nodes with 4 or more PSUs. High-density liquid racks with CDU manifolds. InfiniBand rails (two leafs per node) and front-end Ethernet cabling.

7.10.1Scope

  • In (datacenter-specific extensions to the Inventory record):
  • hall kind;
  • rows (paired face to face around a cold aisle);
  • rack grid position, rack budget kW, rack type and condition;
  • device U position and height;
  • components (ports, power ports with their outlet, management, console, module bays);
  • inventory items such as optics;
  • power panels (a hall's A / B busway or −48 V distribution) and feeds (rack PDUs);
  • PSU cords to PDU outlets;
  • data cables with their structured route;
  • device and module types with component templates;
  • rack-unit reservations;
  • floor plan colourings;
  • elevation drag-to-move;
  • the Explorer;
  • DCIM change control.
  • Out:
  • the asset master record: serial, model, purchase, warranty and lifecycle state (Plan to Build Inventory);
  • change approval workflow (Services & ITSM Change Management);
  • IP address records (Plan to Build IP Management; the DCIM device drawer only reads and assigns through it);
  • purchase of devices (Supply Chain).

BRULE-DCIM-02Device status is one of: Active, Planned, Maintenance, Decommissioning, Tenant-owned. A Planned device reserves its U range and its type's typical power.
BRULE-DCIM-03U occupancy is checked on install and move: no clash with devices, the ODF block or reservations, and the device must fit within the rack height.
BRULE-DCIM-04Power: each rack PDU side (A / B) is rated at the rack budget. Σ PDU load = rack draw, and Σ feeds on a panel = panel load. Odd PSUs go to A and even PSUs to B. A new feed's rating = V × A × 0.8 (× √3 when three-phase), the 80 % continuous rating. Panel A of a hall is fed by one UPS module (UPS-2 / 4) and panel B by another (UPS-1 / 3).
BRULE-DCIM-05A single point of failure is flagged when a device has both cords on one side, a PSU has failed, a PDU is tripped, or a cabinet is at ≥ 90 % of budget (one side cannot carry it).
BRULE-DCIM-06Cabling (TIA-942): port → optic → patch cord → cabinet panel → trunk (2 tray routes; Tier II = 1) → ODF → cross-connect → far end. In-rack = one cord. Backhaul adds the outside-plant core. Cables = every LLDP link + every power cord.
BRULE-DCIM-07Connect: only a free port to a free port of the same speed at the same site. Disconnect frees both ends.
BRULE-DCIM-08Every DCIM edit except the catalogue runs under a change request. There are three options:<br>• Standard (pre-approved record work: patch cords, space holds, port label / enable, hot-swap PSU or fan) is raised and closed at once as a Standard change on template STD-07 "DCIM record:" (STD-02 / STD-05 for cords and hot swaps). It is refused at a site under a freeze that allows emergency changes only (an open Critical incident).<br>• Open CR at the site: Approved / In progress = applied now; Draft / Pending = saved as planned work.<br>• Raise new: a change request in Pending approval, origin DCIM.
BRULE-DCIM-09Planned work runs once its change is approved and not blocked by policy. A DCIM-raised change completes when all its work is done. Records are tagged with the change id (rack, device, interface, module, reservation, panel, feed), and audit lines carry it.
BRULE-DCIM-10A rack can be retired only when it is empty. A reserved U range is respected by install and move until it is released.
BRULE-DCIM-11A planned device that was recorded before DCIM change control carries the open delivery change request of its rack. Records touched by a change are tagged (rack, device, port, module slot, reservation, panel, feed) and every audit line names the change.
BRULE-DCIM-12Invariants checked on every data change: racks and devices equal the estate; Σ hall = site; rows partition a hall's racks; a rack's U = Σ devices + the ODF block, with no U clash; Σ feeds = rack draw; Σ panel = Σ feeds; cables = LLDP links + power cords; Σ devices per type = devices.

7.11Compliance

Purpose. This module checks the estate continuously against operating policies, and maps the policy results to the controls of the standards the operator is audited on.
